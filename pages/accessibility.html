Design & Access

Accessibility Statement

I designed this site to be easier to navigate, read, and understand across devices.

Specific changes I made

Clear headings

I organized pages with a clear title and section headings so the structure is easier to skim and follow.

Consistent navigation

Each major page links back to the homepage, gallery, reflection, AI-use page, accessibility page, sustainability page, and about page.

Readable layout

I used larger type, high contrast text, spacious cards, and shorter sections so the site does not feel crowded.

Responsive design

The card grid adjusts on smaller screens, so the site works better on both laptops and phones.

Images and media

For images and GIFs, I tried to provide surrounding text that explains what the artifact shows and why it matters. For the final version of the site, each image should also have descriptive alt text. Good alt text should explain the meaning of the image, not only the file name.

Example:

Instead of “comic image,” better alt text would be: “Panel from a Tết comic showing a child receiving lucky money during Lunar New Year, used to explore how AI image generation handles cultural storytelling.”

Known limitations

Some external tools, such as Google My Maps, Kumu, Google Drive, and Suno, may have accessibility limits I cannot fully control. When possible, I provide written explanations, screenshots, or direct links so the project is still understandable even if the embed does not work well.
