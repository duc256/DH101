# Week 1 Reflection

Reflect 1: What counts as “making” in the age of AI? What might be gained/lost when machines participate in making?

In the age of AI, "making" changes from humans creating something entirely by themselves to collaborating with machines to produce art, code, music, writing, designs, etc. For instance, in coding field, a programmer uses AI to draft a function or explain an error, then tests it, fixes edges cases, and integrates it into a larger project. "Making" here is debugging, validating and designing the system. With the help of machines in making, we gain speed, accessibility, and new forms of creativity. AI can help people with less technical skills create complex things, lower barriers to entry, and allow creators to experiment more freely. It also supports creativity by generating ideas and patterns that humans might not think on their own, leading to new styles. For example, a beginner can build a simple app faster with AI help. However, there are drawbacks. The process of struggling, practicing, and learning through making may be less visible or valued. If creators rely heavily on AI, they may lose authorship, skill development, or emotional connection to what they make. It is undeniable that if a person relies on AI for all coding and stops learning fundamental, the skill growth will slow down.
