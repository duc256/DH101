# Week 11 – AI & Labor

## The Zine/Infographic
Title: The Hidden Workers behind AI


## Research Notes
For this project, I focused on data labeling as a hidden form of labor behind AI systems. Data labeling includes tasks such as tagging images, sorting text, transcribing audio, and organizing information so that machine learning systems can recognize patterns and make predictions. I used Marie Hicks’s article Hacking the Cis-tem: Transgender Citizens and the Early Digital State as a key source because it helped me think about how technological systems are not neutral. Hicks shows that systems are built with assumptions and power structures inside them, and that they can make some forms of labor and some groups of people invisible while presenting the system as normal or objective. That idea helped me connect hidden AI labor to a broader history of exclusion and inequality in technology.

I also used the idea of “ghost work” from Mary Gray and Siddharth Suri to understand how many data labelers work through digital platforms with low pay, little security, and limited recognition. The hardest part of this research was finding exact information about wages and conditions, because this labor is often outsourced and hidden from the public. That difficulty actually reinforced the main point of my project: this work is meant to stay invisible.

What surprised me most was how much AI depends on human workers. Before doing this project, I mostly thought of AI as a technical achievement. After this research, I see it more as a labor system. AI may look automatic, but it still depends on people doing repetitive and underpaid work behind the scenes.

## Design Statement
I chose to create an infographic because it is the clearest and most accessible format for this topic. Since my project is about invisible labor, I wanted a design that could quickly make hidden information visible. An infographic works well because it lets me combine short explanations, key ideas, and simple visuals in a format that is easy to read and share. I also wanted something that a viewer could understand in a short amount of time without needing to read several pages of text.

My intended audience is students and everyday users of AI tools. Many people use AI, social media, and digital platforms without thinking about the labor behind them. I wanted to create something that would help people stop and question the common idea that AI is automatic or independent. The structure of my infographic moves from definition, to workers, to conditions, to profit, so the viewer can clearly see the system of extraction.

I want my audience to feel surprised, unsettled, and more aware. I want them to realize that AI is not just a technical system, but also a labor system built on hidden human work. More than anything, I want viewers to remember that the people who make AI possible are often the least visible and the least rewarded. My goal is to encourage more critical thinking about fairness, transparency, and responsibility in the development and use of AI technologies.
