This project is a short interactive story built in Twine titled The Prompt Engineer. The player interacts with an AI system by writing different types of prompts in order to generate a meaningful story. Each choice leads to a different type of response from the AI, shaping the experience and outcome.


## Design Statement
My game, The Prompt Engineer, explores the relationship between human intention and AI-generated output. The player’s goal is to generate a meaningful story by interacting with an AI system through prompts. However, instead of rewarding perfect control, the game shows how meaning becomes unstable when filtered through AI systems.

The key mechanic of the game is the choice between different prompting strategies, such as being specific, vague, or creative. These choices matter because they produce different types of outputs. A highly structured prompt leads to clean but lifeless results. A vague prompt leads to unstable and confusing responses. A creative prompt produces something more interesting, but still imperfect. Through these variations, the player begins to see that prompting is not equivalent to control.

The game uses procedural rhetoric by making the player experience this limitation directly. Instead of telling the player that AI cannot fully understand human intention, the system demonstrates it through repeated interaction. Even the most optimized path leads to an unsatisfying or empty result. This shifts the focus away from “winning” and toward reflection.

The structure of the game is intentionally simple, with a small number of passages and branching choices. This keeps the player focused on the core tension between control and interpretation. By limiting the player’s actions to prompt selection, the game emphasizes how human agency is mediated and constrained when interacting with AI systems.

## Artist Statement
This project changed how I think about games and creativity. Before this assignment, I mostly saw games as spaces for freedom and exploration. While making this game, I realized that rules and constraints are what actually create meaning. The choices I designed did not just move the story forward. They shaped how the player understands the system they are interacting with.

My collaboration with AI was supportive rather than generative in a full sense. I used AI to help brainstorm the concept, organize the structure, and revise wording for clarity. However, I made all final decisions about the theme, the branching choices, and the emotional direction of the game. I edited and adjusted the language so it would better reflect the idea I wanted to communicate.

One of the most important things I learned is that AI can make writing feel easier, but it can also flatten meaning if used too heavily. The most interesting parts of the game came from deciding what to keep, what to remove, and how to shape the player’s experience. If I did this project again, I would expand the branching paths and explore more consequences for each type of interaction with the AI. I would also experiment with giving the player more ways to resist or disrupt the system.
Overall, this project helped me understand that games are not just stories with choices. They are systems that create meaning through rules, structure, and player experience.
